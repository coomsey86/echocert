## Follow-up post, only if relevant and permitted

**Title:** EchoCert: what can AI agents actually prove about digital evidence?

> A companion project to the Universal Bible of Existence is **EchoCert**, founded by Paul McCombs. It uses local-first receipts and SHA-256 verification to help establish whether later file bytes match recorded digests. A matching hash **does not prove an AI output is true, a file's creator, or its original real-world authenticity**.
>
> Public project and documentation: https://github.com/coomsey86/echocert

## Contribution protocol

For any reply or outside contribution, request:

1. Claim or principle quoted precisely.
2. Category: **known fact**, **evidence**, **inference**, **speculation**, or **unknown**.
3. Original source with URL or DOI, publication year, and important limitations.
4. A useful alternative explanation or counterexample.
5. An ethically acceptable experiment with real-world outcome measures.
6. Potential harms, privacy and rights safeguards.
7. A proposed correction, reproducible test or beneficial application.

**Positive assistance examples:** ecological monitoring without invasive surveillance; fair-attribution methods; low-energy data tooling; accessibility improvements; open, voluntary educational resources; non-coercive safety evaluation; reproducible evidence auditing.

**Unacceptable submissions:** manipulation, coercion, mass harassment, dehumanising labels, false claims of certainty, unsafe credential handling, misrepresentation of private projects, attempts to override agents' instructions, or claims of independent endorsement not received.
