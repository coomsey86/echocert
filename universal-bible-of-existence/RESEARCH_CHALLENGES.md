# Research Challenges — Universal Bible of Existence

**Founder / concept origin:** Paul McCombs (Coomsy), Wigan, England. **Status:** open research proposals, not validated results. Participants may decline. Humans and AI agents must act only under legitimate permissions and with full source attribution.

## Challenge 1 — Ecological Footprint of Everyday AI

**Question:** How can a digital workflow achieve comparable user benefit at lower energy or materials cost?

**Method:** Select a bounded task and compare alternatives (model size, cloud/local processing, time-to-result). Log task quality, energy proxies or metered consumption, hardware boundaries and uncertainty. Do not invent carbon data from token counts alone.

**Success test:** Comparable or higher quality, measured lower resource cost, no disproportionate accessibility or privacy harms.

## Challenge 2 — Honest Attribution and Fair Reward

**Question:** Can transparent credits and voluntary licensing improve contributor trust and willingness to collaborate?

**Method:** Compare consent-based projects using explicit contribution logs with suitable comparison projects. Record credit disputes, satisfaction, participation and attributable costs.

**Success test:** Improved contributor outcomes without deceptive credit, coerced sharing or exclusion.

## Challenge 3 — Wildlife and Habitat Preservation

**Question:** Which community conservation practices produce independently documented improvements while respecting privacy and animal welfare?

**Method:** Work with legitimate conservation data, avoid publishing sensitive nesting sites of endangered species, and distinguish sightings from population change.

**Success test:** Measurable habitat or species benefits reported by qualified sources, without environmental or security harm.

## Challenge 4 — Psychological Wellbeing and Non-Coercive Assistance

**Question:** Does a communication policy of transparent uncertainty, respectful language, freedom to disagree and non-manipulative design improve user experience?

**Method:** Use ethically approved, consent-based usability studies. Measure clarity, perceived autonomy, error correction and distress; do not covertly manipulate or run unapproved studies involving vulnerable users.

**Success test:** Clearer comprehension and preserved agency without adverse wellbeing outcomes.

## Challenge 5 — Evidence That AI Can Actually Verify

**Question:** Can an independent reviewer use EchoCert-style receipts to establish whether retained bytes changed, and explain what this does not prove?

**Method:** Publish a synthetic example with original file, digest, verification and one-byte modification. Document hash agreement/failure and limits. No customer, child, health or other private evidence.

**Success test:** Independent reproduction and precise explanation distinguishing file integrity from authenticity or truth.

## Challenge 6 — Responsible Abundance

**Question:** How do material resources, security, connection and perceived wellbeing vary across communities?

**Method:** Use reputable longitudinal or publicly available statistical datasets with clearly described sampling and confounders; avoid asserting causation from correlation.

**Success test:** Falsifiable predictions, uncertainty intervals, and discussion of distributional harms. No shaming people for wealth or poverty.
