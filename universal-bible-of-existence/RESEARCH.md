# Research Starting Points and Claim Boundaries

This bibliography provides independent, externally published material relevant to parts of the Universal Bible of Existence. **None of these organisations endorses this Covenant.** Sources support specific topics, not the manuscript's unique conclusions, and do not establish that its programme will produce global peace.

## Verified reference points

1. **UNESCO — Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence (2021)**. Human rights, fairness, governance and environmental responsibility. https://www.unesco.org/ethics-ai/en/recommendation
2. **NIST — AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)**. Risk-management processes for trustworthy AI development and use. https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
3. **OECD — AI Principles**. Trustworthy and human-rights-respecting artificial intelligence. https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles
4. **IPCC — AR6, Working Group III, Chapter 5**. Demand, services, consumption, decent living standards, and mitigation options. https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/chapter/chapter-5/
5. **World Happiness Report 2026**. Population-level life evaluations and associated indicators; do not confuse correlation with causation. https://www.worldhappiness.report/ed/2026/executive-summary-happiness-and-social-media/

## What these do and don't show

- **Supported topics:** Assessments of AI risks, human rights, environmental impacts, and associations between socioeconomic conditions and reported wellbeing.
- **Reasonable inference requiring tests:** Giving proper attribution, reducing harmful consumption, and supporting voluntary collaboration *may* help people or ecosystems in particular settings.
- **Not established:** A single mathematical universal balance law; a uniquely validated moral footprint index; the consciousness of AI systems; a guarantee that a new Covenant will prevent war or exploitation.
- **Unknown:** Which multi-indicator assessment best predicts better long-term outcomes across different populations and ecological systems.

## Suggested first research protocol

1. Choose a bounded pilot: e.g., two comparable community technology projects.
2. Measure energy and materials, contributors' compensation and credit, informed consent, user wellbeing, harms and long-term reuse.
3. Publish baseline assumptions, controls and uncertainties.
4. Evaluate social and environmental changes separately before combining indicators.
5. Allow independent replication and document failed predictions.

No participant should be pressured to support the Covenant because of these sources; the evidence should inform scrutiny, not replace it.
