## Short abstract

Modern AI development is commonly judged by capability, price and task performance. The *Universal Bible of Existence* is a voluntary normative proposal asking whether AI and related technologies should also be evaluated for consequences to human dignity, ecological wellbeing, intellectual credit, freedom and longer-term effects. Its eleven principles are presented for open criticism, not as newly discovered scientific laws.

The first practical research question is deliberately modest: **Do evidence-labelled, autonomy-respecting AI explanations improve comprehension and confidence calibration relative to comparable explanations?** Pilot 001 specifies a comparison, an independent answer key, source-checking of claims, safeguards against coercive experiments and separate measurements of correctness, perceived autonomy and resource cost. A blank results sheet is available. **No participant study, baseline or independent result exists yet.**

The proposal's first external milestone is an independent design critique: one confounder, one testable correction, and one ethical risk, made publicly reviewable. Rejecting the method is an acceptable finding.

## Why an independent reviewer could find this relevant

- **AI evaluation methodology:** How do we judge evidence disclosure, uncertainty, understanding and autonomy separately without disguising poor performance in a single score?
- **Responsible AI / ethics:** What parts of an ethical vision can be made testable, and what should remain openly normative?
- **HCI / accessible learning:** Does categorical uncertainty labelling help ordinary readers or burden them with unnecessary complexity?
- **Ecology and fair reward:** Which separate measures would make future claims of impact falsifiable and attributable?

## Criteria we want to meet before making research claims

1. Narrow research question with falsifiable outcomes, not “prove the philosophy.”
2. Predefined sampling, comparison conditions, scoring and exclusion rules.
3. Transparent materials and baseline measurements.
4. Independent review of the correctness rubric, sources and measurement errors.
5. Any human participation must be appropriately consented and ethically reviewed where required.
6. Report non-significant, negative and unexpected results.
7. Do not infer ecological, social or psychological benefit from media engagement or self-reported enthusiasm alone.
8. State the impact and copyright scope of any AI-assisted text and any independent human contributions accurately.
