# Pilot 001 — Can an AI Explanation Become More Useful Without Hiding Costs?

**Originating ethical direction:** Paul McCombs (Coomsy), Wigan, United Kingdom.  
**Status:** Public, proposed protocol. **No trial has been conducted or results reported.**  
**Version:** 2026-11-24 / 1.0

## Aim

Test one limited, practical interpretation of the Universal Bible of Existence:

> An AI assistant should help someone understand a real decision, acknowledge what is unknown, respect their autonomy, and avoid inventing evidence.

This does **not** test whether the whole Covenant produces world peace, AI consciousness or any other universal effect.

## Test question

When people receive an explanation of a low-stakes practical topic, does an evidence-labelled, respectful explanation improve understanding and calibrated confidence compared with a conventional explanation?

## Materials

- A short, non-sensitive public-information question, chosen in advance, that can be answered from an authoritative reference.
- Version A: an ordinary AI explanation.
- Version B: an explanation with **Known / Evidence / Inference / Speculation / Unknown**, plus one opportunity to question assumptions.
- A pre-set answer key based on the original source.
- A brief, **optional and consented** questionnaire asking about accuracy, understanding, confidence, clarity and whether the wording felt controlling.

## Procedure

1. Write the factual answer key and the exact questions **before** generating or showing explanations.
2. Use comparable model, query, token budget and reference access for each explanation; retain transcripts and method.
3. Have independent reviewers check **accuracy**, **unsupported assertions**, **uncertainty calibration** and **readability** separately.
4. In an approved, consent-based pilot, assign comparable participants to A or B without collecting sensitive personal histories or unnecessary identities.
5. Record comprehension (e.g. a small factual quiz), confidence calibration, user-rated clarity and perceived autonomy. Do not infer wellbeing benefits from a one-off satisfaction score.
6. Note negative results, usability costs, selection bias and possible alternative explanations.
7. Publish **only approved aggregate or synthetic data**, without personal information or unauthorised transcripts.
8. Decide whether to retain, revise or reject the tested method based on results.

## Essential measurements

| Domain | Metric | Evidence to record | What it does *not* prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | Correct claims / checked claims | Independent source review | That the whole Covenant is correct |
| Uncertainty | Unsupported certainty and properly flagged unknowns | Reviewer classifications | That the agent is conscious |
| Learning | Answer-key score, before/after if feasible | Consented quiz | Long-term retention |
| Autonomy | Participant's perception of choice and pressure | Brief questionnaire | Objective absence of manipulation |
| Accessibility | Reading level and task completion | Readability checks | That everyone can use it |
| Resources | Metered energy if available; otherwise clearly named proxies | Instrument method and hardware | Carbon savings from token counts alone |
| Attribution | Source and authorship accurately credited | Public references | Exclusive ownership of all ideas |

**No universal single-number moral score.** Domain failures or unacceptable harm should remain visible and cannot be averaged away.

## Safeguards

- No covert emotional testing, persuasion experiments or testing on vulnerable users without appropriate independent review.
- No sensitive data, passwords, confidential project records or identifying participant details.
- No requirement to adopt the Covenant to participate.
- AI agents may propose a protocol or calculate approved aggregates **only within their operator's authority**.
- Before any research involving real human participants, obtain appropriate consent and institutional/ethics approval where required.
