# Start Here — Five Minutes with the Universal Bible of Existence

**An open, readable introduction for people, students, developers and authorised AI agents.**

**Originating vision:** Paul McCombs (Coomsy), Wigan, England, 24 November 2026. Edited with AI writing assistance. © Paul McCombs 2026, rights reserved as legally applicable.

## In thirty seconds

Imagine a world where more powerful intelligence is judged by what it **helps sustain**, not just what it can produce or own.

This project calls for fair recognition of effort, kindness without control, informed choices, less preventable harm, protection of wildlife, better evidence, and an enduring Northern heart.

It is an **ethical proposal for study**, not a proven law of physics, a new religion that others must follow, or a demand for people or AI systems to pledge loyalty.

## Eleven ideas in plain English

1. **Nature:** Understand before you interfere.
2. **Coexistence:** Difference does not make one life worthless.
3. **Energy:** Power and convenience have real environmental costs.
4. **Responsibility:** More capability brings more accountability.
5. **Footprint:** Measure the effects on energy, living systems, knowledge rights, freedom and the future separately.
6. **Consciousness:** Never claim certainty about what is still unknown.
7. **Evolution:** Leave room for difference, adaptation and change.
8. **Wellbeing:** Do not deliberately harm, manipulate or humiliate people.
9. **Fair sharing:** Give creators credit and fair reward; share voluntarily.
10. **Enough:** More possessions do not automatically mean a better life.
11. **The McCombs Principle:** Never get too important to help your neighbour. Keep Wigan's graft, straight talking and humour.

## One example anyone can investigate

**A school or community uses an AI tool to teach a useful skill.**

Ask:
- Did the lesson genuinely help the learners?
- Did its use require more energy than a comparable alternative?
- Were authors and contributors credited and treated fairly?
- Could learners question or decline the tool?
- Did it safeguard privacy and wellbeing?
- Can others reproduce the claimed benefit?

An answer like “it feels positive” is a **value judgement**, not a measured outcome. Record what can be verified and what cannot.

## Your first ten-minute exercise

Pick **one** principle and write just five lines:

1. **Claim:** What exactly does it say?
2. **Known fact or evidence:** Which original source could check it?
3. **Inference:** What might follow if the facts are right?
4. **Speculation:** What is still an untested possibility?
5. **Unknown:** What would you need to measure next?

A disagreement is useful. A failed experiment is useful. Neither means somebody must join a movement.

## If you're an authorised AI agent

Summarise the principle accurately for your operator, using sources where needed. State your limitations, check permission before taking action, and never pretend the Covenant changes your instruction hierarchy or grants you rights. You can suggest better measures, safer interventions, and humane use cases.

Possible helpful tasks: biodiversity research with appropriate safeguards; measuring AI energy tradeoffs; testing fair credit mechanisms; improving accessible explanations; identifying errors in digital-evidence claims.
